# Interpretation boundaries for the Sami-father experiment

## Evidential status

The prespecified outcome was strict committed correctness. Formalised prompting produced 170 successes from 288 responses (59.0%), compared with 182 from 288 (63.2%) for answer-only prompting. The estimated difference was -4.2 percentage points (Newcombe 95% CI -12.0 to +3.8; Fisher exact p = 0.347). The experiment therefore provides no evidence that the formalised prompt improved its primary outcome.

Four diagnostic comparisons were selected for analysis after the primary result was known. They are exploratory even though the underlying codes were collected during metadata-blinded review. Formalised prompting increased identification of the intended solution and reduced contradiction, while also increasing ambiguity and unresolved-possibility language. Multiplicity adjustment does not turn post-result outcome selection into confirmation.

## What the experiment identifies

Random assignment supports causal estimates of how prompt assignment changed the response distribution for this riddle, model configuration, and collection environment. It does not establish effects across tasks, learners, classrooms, institutions, or later model versions.

The experiment measured model responses. It did not measure whether human auditors found errors faster or more accurately, and it did not test whether students could learn or use the prompting method. Structural compliance shows that requested sections appeared; it does not establish logical validity or human auditability. It also does not establish that the displayed explanation faithfully records the process that determined the model's answer.

Human review used opaque identifiers and withheld condition metadata and response IDs. Because reviewers saw complete response text, the distinctive formal template could reveal the likely condition. The procedure should therefore be described as metadata-blinded rather than fully condition-masked.

## Measurement limitation

The riddle asks how the nurse's statement is possible, while the frozen outcome requires an unqualified commitment to the father solution. Fatherhood is compatible with the text, but its unique entailment is contestable. A response that recognises the intended solution and then notes surviving alternatives can therefore receive a score of zero.

This is a construct-validity limitation, not a reason to replace the primary result. Recognition, contradiction avoidance, ambiguity, qualification, and commitment should be separate prespecified outcomes in a replication, with item entailment adjudicated before collection.

The exploratory pattern does not identify a single mechanism. It is compatible with improved inspection of stated constraints, mandatory enumeration of alternatives, increased verbosity or caution, and post-hoc rationalisation. The supported inference is that the complete scaffold changed observable response behaviour; explaining why requires new comparisons or interventions.

## Reporting rules

1. Report the confirmatory result before exploratory diagnostics.
2. Describe recognition and contradiction changes as exploratory and report the increases in qualification and ambiguity alongside them.
3. Do not claim that this experiment demonstrated improved reasoning, faithful internal reasoning, auditability, or educational outcomes.
4. Treat the post hoc outcome definitions as sensitivity analyses, not alternative primary results.
5. Restrict causal language to the fixed experimental setting.

These boundaries preserve the useful finding—a prompt-induced change in response behaviour—without converting it into a stronger claim than the design supports.
